#Message from Captain

### 1. First-Year Applied Engineering Guest Lectures
* **Proposed Activities:** Deliver dedicated guest application modules in high-enrollment first-year undergraduate courses (e.g., Classical Mechanics, Linear Algebra, Integral Calculus, and Introductory Circuits). Lectures connect foundational math and physics concepts directly to active CubeSat engineering problems, such as attitude determination algorithms, orbital decay simulations, and solar array power budgeting.
* **Target Audience:** First-year Queen’s University engineering and science undergraduates.
* **Promotional Tools:** Announcements through Queen’s learning management system (onQ), departmental email lists, and instructor syllabus integration.
* **Anticipated Reach & Expected Outcomes:** 500+ students reached annually across multiple class sections. Demonstrates immediate real-world utility of abstract coursework, reinforces STEM retention, and establishes an equitable early recruitment touchpoint across the university.

### 2. Regional High School Space School & High-Altitude Balloon Launch
* **Proposed Activities:** Expand the Space School training model into a structured outreach program for regional secondary schools. Student cohorts complete guided micro-avionics and sensor labs, culminating in designing, building, and launching custom payloads to ~30 km altitude via high-altitude weather balloons.
* **Target Audience:** Secondary students in Grades 10–12, specifically targeting rural and under-resourced secondary schools across Eastern Ontario.
* **Promotional Tools:** Direct partnerships with regional school boards (LDSB and ALCDSB), targeted high school STEM educator networks, and outreach documentation packages.
* **Anticipated Reach & Expected Outcomes:** 30–50 direct high school payload builders per launch cycle, with 500+ students engaged via preliminary classroom demonstrations. Fosters early technical competencies (soldering, microcontroller programming), demystifies aerospace engineering pathways, and creates an accessible post-secondary pipeline.

### 3. Graduate Pedagogy Research & Open-Access Online Training Modules
* **Proposed Activities:** Support graduate-level engineering education research focused on design team pedagogy to formalize the Space School curriculum into a modular, self-paced, open-access online training course. These modules cover CubeSat subsystems, clean-room practices, and systems engineering workflows.
* **Target Audience:** Canadian post-secondary engineering students, self-directed learners, and educators bootstrapping student satellite teams.
* **Promotional Tools:** Open-access institutional repositories, academic engineering education conferences (e.g., CEEA), and Canadian student space consortium networks.
* **Anticipated Reach & Expected Outcomes:** 1,000+ online module completions across multiple Canadian institutions over the project lifecycle. Creates a permanent, sustainable pedagogical framework that remains active and accessible beyond individual student graduations.

### 4. Open-Source Aerospace Repositories & Design Packages
* **Proposed Activities:** Release flight hardware schematics, PCB layout files (KiCad), modular flight firmware, and systems documentation under open-source licenses on GitHub. Publicly maintain detailed Interface Control Documents (ICDs) and Concept of Operations (CONOPS) as educational reference architectures.
* **Target Audience:** Collegiate CubeSat design teams, independent makers, open-source developers, and early-stage aerospace research labs.
* **Promotional Tools:** Public GitHub repositories, dedicated Read the Docs sites, open-source aerospace working groups, and student space network forums.
* **Anticipated Reach & Expected Outcomes:** Hundreds of code and design package downloads nationwide. Lowers technical and financial entry barriers for emerging Canadian university satellite programs by eliminating redundant subsystem design efforts.

### 5. Science Rendezvous Kingston Interactive Showcase
* **Proposed Activities:** Host interactive, public-facing demonstration booths at Kingston’s annual Science Rendezvous festival, featuring satellite engineering test models, clean-room simulation kits, and real-time telemetry decoding demonstrations.
* **Target Audience:** K–12 youth, families, educators, and members of the broader Kingston community.
* **Promotional Tools:** Official Science Rendezvous program promotion, Queen's research communications, local media releases, and interactive physical hardware exhibits.
* **Anticipated Reach & Expected Outcomes:** 3,500–5,000 in-person attendees engaged annually. Increases public awareness of Canadian Space Agency contributions and excites youth about domestic aerospace capabilities.

### 6. Amateur Radio (Ham Radio) Telemetry Network & Community Decoding
* **Proposed Activities:** Publish the satellite's RF downlink parameters, packet framing formats, and software-defined radio (SDR) decoding flowgraphs to enable amateur radio operators worldwide to track the satellite, receive health beacons, and submit telemetry packets.
* **Target Audience:** Canadian and international amateur radio operators, citizen scientists, and collegiate communications clubs.
* **Promotional Tools:** Notices submitted to AMSAT (Radio Amateur Satellite Corporation) bulletins, amateur radio forums, open packet repositories (such as SatNOGS), and club presentations.
* **Anticipated Reach & Expected Outcomes:** 100+ global ground stations actively receiving and logging beacon data, providing crowdsourced satellite health tracking while engaging citizen scientists directly in mission operations.

### 7. Mission Milestone Livestreams & Public Watch Parties
* **Proposed Activities:** Broadcast live virtual and in-person showcase events during pivotal mission milestones, including FlatSat integration completion, pre-flight delivery to the Canadian Space Agency, orbital deployment, and initial beacon acquisition. Broadcasts include plain-language technical explanations and live Q&A sessions with student leads.
* **Target Audience:** University alumni, Queen’s student body, sponsor organizations, secondary school cohorts, and the general public.
* **Promotional Tools:** Queen's social media channels, faculty-wide mailing lists, press releases via local news outlets, and university communications features.
* **Anticipated Reach & Expected Outcomes:** ~100 live and on-demand views per major milestone event. Humanizes student-led engineering research, celebrates Canadian technical milestones, and creates transparent public visibility into government-funded space initiatives.

### 8. Canadian Inter-Collegiate Ground Station Sharing Network
* **Proposed Activities:** Coordinate a federated ground station sharing initiative among participating Canadian university CubeSat teams (interfacing via standardized open networks such as SatNOGS or shared scheduler APIs). Teams cross-schedule tracking passes across different Canadian longitudes and latitudes, sharing downlink capacity and ground coverage.
* **Target Audience:** Canadian university CubeSat design teams, university ground station operators, and academic aerospace research groups nationwide.
* **Promotional Tools:** Canadian student space conferences, direct inter-university technical working groups, shared GitHub repository integrations, and consortium coordination calls.
* **Anticipated Reach & Expected Outcomes:** 5–8 Canadian post-secondary institutions interconnected into the network. Multiplies total contact time and downlink data yield for all participating Canadian CubeSat missions, builds inter-provincial student collaboration, and maximizes the collective return on university ground infrastructure.
